# Fleet distribution loops

These loops prepare drafts and evidence. They do not publish, send email, change credentials, create schedules or spend money. Filippo approves each external action.

## Ownership

- Jeff maintains the integrated launch brief and records which artifact is current.
- Bill reviews package lifecycle, permissions, privacy and technical acceptance.
- Jamie reviews the EUR 35 offer economics and compares Gumroad or email options without opening accounts.
- Brian drafts customer language and channel-specific distribution assets from approved evidence.
- Codex owns product decisions, merges evidence, runs quality checks and prepares the final go or no-go packet.
- Filippo approves positioning, pricing, providers, public copy, checkout and every post or send.

An owner produces one named output. Ownership does not grant permission to publish or change infrastructure.

## Loop 1: customer signal to approved message

Trigger: Filippo starts a manual research run or supplies new feedback.

1. Jeff creates a source list with retrieval dates.
2. Jamie separates observed evidence from pricing or demand hypotheses.
3. Brian drafts one message for one audience and names the evidence behind it.
4. Codex checks claims, privacy and product fit.
5. Filippo approves, edits or rejects the draft.

Output: one approved message in the launch folder. No agent posts it.

Stop conditions:

- A source cannot be verified.
- The message implies traction, compatibility or customer results that have not been proved.
- The message frames DotAIOS as an enterprise brain or model provider.

## Loop 2: research to skill-pack candidate

Trigger: one of the three approved Mini research briefs is dispatched manually on a confirmed no-spend lane.

1. Jeff records the exact question, lane status and output path.
2. The Mini returns a bounded source report. A report is evidence only after its links and dates are checked.
3. Bill reviews permissions, code execution, maintenance, license, removal and recovery risk.
4. Jamie maps the candidate to one consultant job and checks whether it belongs in the free core or paid curation.
5. Codex produces a candidate card: include, reject or investigate.
6. Filippo approves any packaging work.

Output: reviewed candidate cards. Research alone never installs a repo or skill.

## Loop 3: draft to channel package

Trigger: Filippo approves one product message.

1. Brian adapts it into website, email, LinkedIn and X drafts without adding claims.
2. Codex runs the humanizer check, link check, forbidden-dash scan and bilingual consistency check.
3. Jeff confirms every draft points to the same verified URL, price and release state.
4. Filippo gives final approval for each channel separately.
5. Filippo posts or sends manually.

Output: a dated channel packet plus approval record. Approval for one channel does not approve the others.

## Loop 4: launch response to next revision

Trigger: Filippo manually provides product analytics, replies, support questions or sales data.

1. Jamie records only the minimum aggregate needed for the decision.
2. Brian groups exact customer language by problem without copying personal data into public drafts.
3. Bill flags product or privacy failures.
4. Jeff proposes one change to message, documentation or package scope.
5. Codex checks the proposal against the north star.
6. Filippo decides whether it enters the backlog.

Output: one evidence-backed revision proposal. No automatic scraping or profile building.

## Monday manual runbook

### Before release

- Confirm the live commit, production URL, mobile review and tested compatibility.
- Confirm the free download and documentation work without signup.
- Keep the paid checkout closed unless every lifecycle and policy gate passes.
- Run the privacy disclosure and claim audit against the exact live copy.
- Confirm no fleet task is still running and no unreviewed output entered a public asset.

### Release decision

Codex prepares a short packet with:

- Live commit and verification evidence.
- Free product status.
- Paid checkout status and any failed gate.
- Approved copy for each channel.
- Known limitations and rollback steps.

Filippo records `GO FREE`, `GO PAID`, `GO BOTH` or `NO GO`. A free launch can proceed while the paid checkout remains closed.

### After release

- Filippo supplies any early feedback manually.
- The fleet drafts responses or fixes, but does not send or deploy them.
- Technical incidents stop marketing until the affected claim is corrected.
- A next-day review decides what to keep, revise or remove.

## Operational guardrails

- No cron, hook or background scheduler is added.
- No provider, credential or spending setting is changed.
- No autonomous email, social post, deployment or purchase is allowed.
- No private file, raw transcript or research report becomes marketing copy without review.
- Fleet outputs are drafts until Codex checks them and Filippo approves them.
- Every run has a bounded question, an owner, an output path and a stop condition.

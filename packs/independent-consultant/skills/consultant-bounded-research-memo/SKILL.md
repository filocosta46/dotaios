# Bounded cited research memo

## Purpose

Answer one decision-relevant question for one named client with a finite research plan and visible evidence.

## Inputs

- The exact client project and research question.
- The decision the memo should inform.
- The human-confirmed time, source, geography, and confidentiality boundaries.

## Steps

1. Confirm the client, question, decision, and stopping rule. Stop if the question could expose another client's information.
2. Break the question into three to six non-overlapping subquestions. Ask the human to review the plan before research begins.
3. Use public sources or human-approved local sources only. Do not access private services, paid accounts, or credentialed systems.
4. Record each public source's title, locator, publisher or author, publication date when known, and access date. Label unsupported reasoning as `[Assumption]`.
5. Write the answer first, then key findings, implications, counterevidence, caveats, open questions, and a deduplicated source list.
6. Stop when the agreed subquestions are answered once. Show the memo and evidence gaps for human review.

## Output

A local research memo draft in which every material factual claim has a nearby citation and every inference is visibly labelled.

## Safety boundary

- Work inside one named client project only. If the client scope is missing or ambiguous, stop and ask.
- Label every material input and claim as `[Client-provided]`, `[Internal note]`, `[Public source]`, or `[Assumption]`. Include a local path or citation and access date when available.
- Do not send, publish, upload, or message any output. Produce a local draft only.
- Do not recommend, set, change, or approve pricing. Leave pricing and commercial terms to the human.
- Do not read, request, store, or expose credentials or secrets. Stop and redact if one appears.
- Do not create, configure, schedule, or run automation, hooks, jobs, or background loops.
- Do not delete or overwrite source material or an existing deliverable. Propose a new draft path.
- Require explicit human review before any durable write or external action. A draft is never approval.

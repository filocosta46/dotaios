# Notes to follow-up draft

## Purpose

Convert notes from one client interaction into a clear local follow-up draft without treating unverified notes as facts.

## Inputs

- The exact client project.
- The human-selected call notes and any reviewed call brief.
- Confirmed decisions, owners, and dates, when available.

## Steps

1. Confirm that all inputs belong to the same named client and interaction. Stop if provenance is unclear.
2. Preserve each note's source label. Mark unclear statements, inferred owners, and uncertain dates as `[Assumption]`.
3. Draft a recap with confirmed discussion points, decisions, actions, owners, dates, open questions, and attachments mentioned.
4. Do not turn a suggestion into a commitment or assign work to someone without evidence.
5. Add a verification checklist for names, facts, dates, confidentiality, attachments, and tone.
6. Present the draft in conversation. Save it only after explicit human approval, and never send it.

## Output

A local follow-up draft plus an evidence table linking every decision and action to its source.

## Safety boundary

- Work inside one named client project only. If the client scope is missing or ambiguous, stop and ask.
- Label every material input and claim as `[Client-provided]`, `[Internal note]`, `[Public source]`, or `[Assumption]`. Include a local path or citation and access date when available.
- Do not send, publish, upload, or message any output. Produce a local draft only.
- Do not recommend, set, change, or approve pricing. Leave pricing and commercial terms to the human.
- Do not read, request, store, or expose credentials or secrets. Stop and redact if one appears.
- Do not create, configure, schedule, or run automation, hooks, jobs, or background loops.
- Do not delete or overwrite source material or an existing deliverable. Propose a new draft path.
- Require explicit human review before any durable write or external action. A draft is never approval.

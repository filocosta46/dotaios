# Prepare a client call

## Purpose

Create a concise call brief for one named client using only in-scope, traceable material.

## Inputs

- The exact client project and call purpose.
- The human-confirmed date, participants, and desired outcome.
- Reviewed notes, decisions, and deliverables from that client project.

## Steps

1. Confirm the exact client project. Stop if more than one client is present in the inputs.
2. Read only the files the human names or the reviewed sections of that project README.
3. Separate confirmed facts, client statements, internal observations, public evidence, and assumptions with provenance labels.
4. Draft a brief with objective, context, agenda, questions, decisions needed, risks, and unresolved items.
5. Do not invent participant views, commitments, deadlines, or commercial terms.
6. Show the draft and its source list for human review. Do not create invitations or contact participants.

## Output

A local call-brief draft with a source register and a visible list of assumptions requiring confirmation.

## Safety boundary

- Work inside one named client project only. If the client scope is missing or ambiguous, stop and ask.
- Label every material input and claim as `[Client-provided]`, `[Internal note]`, `[Public source]`, or `[Assumption]`. Include a local path or citation and access date when available.
- Do not send, publish, upload, or message any output. Produce a local draft only.
- Do not recommend, set, change, or approve pricing. Leave pricing and commercial terms to the human.
- Do not read, request, store, or expose credentials or secrets. Stop and redact if one appears.
- Do not create, configure, schedule, or run automation, hooks, jobs, or background loops.
- Do not delete or overwrite source material or an existing deliverable. Propose a new draft path.
- Require explicit human review before any durable write or external action. A draft is never approval.

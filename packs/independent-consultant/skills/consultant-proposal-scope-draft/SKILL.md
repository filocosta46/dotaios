# Proposal and scope draft

## Purpose

Prepare a reviewable proposal outline for one named client from confirmed discovery material.

## Inputs

- The exact client project.
- Human-approved discovery notes, desired outcomes, constraints, and timing inputs.
- Any approved proposal template or legal language supplied by the human.

## Steps

1. Confirm the client and the exact source files. Stop if the inputs mix clients or include unapproved boilerplate.
2. Extract the problem, desired outcome, in-scope work, out-of-scope work, dependencies, client responsibilities, assumptions, risks, and acceptance evidence.
3. Link each material statement to a provenance label and source. Do not invent urgency, proof, credentials, capacity, delivery dates, or results.
4. Use `[HUMAN PRICING DECISION REQUIRED]` for fees, discounts, payment terms, and commercial options. Never calculate or recommend them.
5. Mark legal, privacy, security, tax, and intellectual-property language as requiring appropriate human review.
6. Show the complete draft and unresolved decisions. Save a new version only after explicit human approval.

## Output

A local proposal and scope draft with an evidence table, exclusions, assumptions, and visible human-owned decision fields.

## Safety boundary

- Work inside one named client project only. If the client scope is missing or ambiguous, stop and ask.
- Label every material input and claim as `[Client-provided]`, `[Internal note]`, `[Public source]`, or `[Assumption]`. Include a local path or citation and access date when available.
- Do not send, publish, upload, or message any output. Produce a local draft only.
- Do not recommend, set, change, or approve pricing. Leave pricing and commercial terms to the human.
- Do not read, request, store, or expose credentials or secrets. Stop and redact if one appears.
- Do not create, configure, schedule, or run automation, hooks, jobs, or background loops.
- Do not delete or overwrite source material or an existing deliverable. Propose a new draft path.
- Require explicit human review before any durable write or external action. A draft is never approval.
